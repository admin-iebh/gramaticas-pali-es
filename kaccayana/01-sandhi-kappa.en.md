**KACCĀYANA-BYĀKARAṆAṂ**  
 **KACCĀYANA'S GRAMMAR**

**Namo Tassa Bhagavato Arahato Sammāsambuddhassa**  
**Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Fully and Perfectly Enlightened One**

**1-SANDHI-KAPPA**  
 **1-SANDHI CHAPTER**

**PAṬHAMA-KAṆḌA**  
**FIRST SECTION**

1) Seṭṭhaṃ tilokamahitaṃ abhivandiy’ aggaṃ,  
    Buddhañ ca Dhammam amalaṃ Gaṇam uttamañ ca,  
    Satthussa tassa vacanatthavaraṃ subuddhuṃ,[^1]  
    Vakkhāmi suttahitam ettha Susandhikappaṃ.[^2]  
   Having saluted the excellent Buddha, honored by the three worlds, and the highest, together with the pure Dhamma, and the noble Order, I will expound here the good chapter on Sandhi which is beneficial to the Word of the Buddha, for the sake of understanding well the noble meaning of the Word of the Teacher.  
2) Seyyaṃ jineritanayena budhā labhanti,  
    Tañ cāpi tassa vacanatthasubodhanena,  
    Atthañ ca akkharapadesu amohabhāvā,  
    Seyyatthiko padam ato vividhaṃ suṇeyya.  
   The wise attained the best[^3] by the way taught by the Victorious One; that way they attained by knowing well the meaning of His word; the meaning they attained by non-confusion of the meaning in letters and words. Therefore, let the one who wishes the best listen to the various \[letters and\] words.

**1\. 1\. Attho akkharasaññāto.** \[Attho \+ akkharasaññāto, 2\]

Sabbavacanānaṃ attho akkhareh’ eva saññāyate. {Akkharavipattiyaṃ|when there is deformity of letters} hi atthassa {dunnayatā|difficulty of understanding} hoti, tasmā {akkharakosallaṃ|skillfulness with letters} bahūpakāraṃ suttantesu.

---

The meaning is known by letters.

The meaning of all words is known only by letters. For when there is deformity of letters, meaning becomes difficult to understand. Therefore, skillfulness with letters is very useful in the Discourses.

---

**2\. 2\. Akkharā p’ ādayo ekacattālīsaṃ (1, 2).** \[Akkharā \+ api \+ a-ādayo \+ ekacattālīsaṃ, 4\]

Te ca kho {akkharā|letters} api {**a**kāradayo|beginning with 'a'} ekacattālīsaṃ suttantesu {sopakārā|very useful}.

Taṃ yathā? a ā i ī u ū e o; ka kha ga gha ṅa; ca cha ja jha ña; ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa; ta tha da dha na; pa pha ba bha ma; ya ra la va sa ha ḷa aṃ. Iti **akkharā** nāma.

Tena kvattho? Attho akkharasaññāto.

---

Letters are forty-one beginning with ‘a’.

And these letters, which are forty-one beginning with ‘a’, are very useful in the Discourses.

Which are these? a ā i ī u ū e o; ka kha ga gha ṅa; ca cha ja jha ña; ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa; ta tha da dha na; pa pha ba bha ma; ya ra la va sa ha ḷa aṃ. These are called “letters”.

What is the benefit of that \[calling them “letters”\]? \[It is beneficial in suttas such as\] “Attho akkharasaññāto” (§1).[^4]

---

**3\. 3\. Tatth’ odantā sarā aṭṭha (3).** \[Tattha \+ o-antā \+ sarā \+ aṭṭha, 4\]

Tattha akkharesu **a**kārādīsu {**o**dantā|ending in 'o'} aṭṭha {akkharā|letters} {sarā|vowels} nāma honti.

Taṃ yathā? a ā i ī u ū e o. Iti **sarā** nāma.

Tena kvattho? Sarā sare {lopaṃ|elision}.

---

Among them, the eight \[letters\] ending in ‘o’ are called “vowels”.

Among those letters, the eight letters beginning with ‘a’ and ending with ‘o’ are called “vowels”.

Which are these? a ā i ī u ū e o. These are called “vowels”.

What is the benefit of that \[calling them “vowels”\]? \[It is beneficial in suttas such as\] “Sarā sare lopaṃ” (§12).

---

**4\. 4\. Lahumattā tayo rassā (4).** \[Lahumattā \+ tayo \+ rassā, 3\]

Tattha aṭṭhasu saresu {lahumattā|light-measured} tayo {sarā|vowels} {rassā|short} nāma honti.

Taṃ yathā? a i u. Iti **rassā** nāma.

Tena kvattho? Rassaṃ.

---

The three light-measured \[vowels\] are short.

Among the eight vowels, the three light-measured \[vowels\] are called “short”.

Which are these? a i u. These are called “short”.

What is the benefit of that \[calling them “short”\]? \[It is beneficial in suttas such as\] “Rassaṃ” (§26).

---

**5\. 5\. Aññe dīghā (5).** \[Aññe \+ dīghā, 2\]

Tattha aṭṭhasu saresu rassehi aññe pañca {sarā|vowels} {dīghā|long} nāma honti.

Taṃ yathā? ā ī ū e o. Iti **dīghā** nāma.

Tena kvattho? Dīghaṃ.

---

The others are long.

Among the eight vowels, the five other vowels apart from the short \[vowels\] are called “long”.

Which are these? ā ī ū e o. These are called “long”.

What is the benefit of that \[calling them “long”\]? \[It is beneficial in suttas such as\] “Dīghaṃ” (§25).

---

**6\. 8\. Sesā byañjanā (6).** \[Sesā \+ byañjanā, 2\]

Ṭhapetvā aṭṭha sare sesā {akkharā|letters} **k**akārādayo **niggahita**ntā {byañjanā|consonants} nāma honti.

Taṃ yathā? ka kha ga gha ṅa; ca cha ja jha ña; ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa; ta tha da dha na; pa pha ba bha ma; ya ra la va sa ha ḷa aṃ. Iti **byañjanā** nāma.

Tena kvattho? Sarā {pakati|original form} byañjane.

---

The remaining are consonants.

Apart from the eight vowels, the remaining letters beginning with ‘ka’ and ending with the niggahita (ṃ) are called “consonants”.

Which are these? ka kha ga gha ṅa; ca cha ja jha ña; ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa; ta tha da dha na; pa pha ba bha ma; ya ra la va sa ha ḷa aṃ. These are called “consonants”.

What is the benefit of that \[calling them “consonants”\]? \[It is beneficial in suttas such as\] “Sarā pakati byañjane” (§23).

---

**7\. 9\. Vaggā pañcapañcaso mantā (7).** \[Vaggā \+ pañcapañcaso \+ ma-antā, 3\]

Tesaṃ kho byañjanānaṃ **k**akārādayo makārantā pañcapañcaso akkharavanto[^5] {vaggā|grouped (groups of five)} nāma honti.

Taṃ yathā? ka kha ga gha ṅa; ca cha ja jha ña; ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa; ta tha da dha na; pa pha ba bha ma. Iti **vaggā** nāma.

Tena kvattho? Vaggantaṃ vā vagge.

---

The \[groups of\] five by five, ending in ‘ma’, are grouped.

Of those consonants, the letters beginning with ‘ka’ and ending with ‘ma’, that are divided into five groups of five letters, are called “grouped”.

Which are these? ka kha ga gha ṅa; ca cha ja jha ña; ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa; ta tha da dha na; pa pha ba bha ma. These are called “grouped”.

What is the benefit of that \[calling them “grouped”\]? \[It is beneficial in suttas such as\] “Vaggantaṃ vā vagge” (§31).

---

**8\. 10\. Aṃ iti niggahitaṃ (8).** \[Aṃ \+ iti \+ niggahitaṃ, 3\]

**Aṃ** iti {**niggahitaṃ**|niggahita (nasalization)} nāma hoti.

Tena kvattho? Aṃ byañjane niggahitaṃ.

---

‘Ṃ’ is the niggahita.

‘Ṃ’ is called niggahita.

What is the benefit of that \[calling it “niggahita”\]? \[It is beneficial in suttas such as\] “Aṃ byañjane niggahitaṃ” (§30).

---

**9\. 11\. Parasamaññā payoge.** \[Para \+ samaññā \+ payoge, 3\]

Yā ca pana paresu sakkataganthesu samaññā {ghosā|voiced} ti vā {aghosā|voiceless} ti vā, tā payoge sati etthāpi yujjante.

Tattha ghosā nāma: ga gha ṅa; ja jha ña; ḍa ḍha ṇa; da dha na; ba bha ma; ya ra la va ha ḷa. Iti **ghosā** nāma. Aghosā nāma: ka kha; ca cha; ṭa ṭha; ta tha; pa pha; sa. Iti **aghosā** nāma.

Tena kvattho? Vagge ghosāghosānaṃ tatiya-paṭhamā.

---

In need, \[are used\] the terms of others.

Those terms \[found\] in the Sanskrit books such as “voiced” or “voiceless”, when there is need, they are also used here.

Among them, the voiced are: ga gha ṅa; ja jha ña; ḍa ḍha ṇa; da dha na; ba bha ma; ya ra la va ha ḷa. These are called “voiced”. The voiceless are: ka kha; ca cha; ṭa ṭha; ta tha; pa pha; sa. These are called “voiceless”.

What is the benefit of that \[calling them “voiced” and “voiceless”\]? \[It is beneficial in suttas such as\] “Vagge ghosāghosānaṃ tatiya-paṭhamā” (§29).

---

**10\. 12\. Pubbam adhoṭhitam assaraṃ sarena viyojaye (28).** \[Pubbaṃ \+ adhoṭhitaṃ \+ assaraṃ \+ sarena \+ viyojaye, 5\]

Tattha sandhiṃ kattukāmo pubbabyañjanaṃ adhoṭhitaṃ assaraṃ katvā sarañ ca upari katvā sarena viyojaye.

Tatr āyam ādi (Khu. i, 67).

---

One should separate the previous vowelless \[consonant\] that lies below from the vowel.

Herein, one who wants to do joining of words should, after making the previous consonant that lies (lit. sits) below, free from the vowel and putting the vowel above, separate the consonant from the vowel.[^6]

\[With the phrase\] “tatr āyam ādi” \[the examples begin\].

---

**11\. 14\. Naye paraṃ yutte (29).** \[Naye \+ paraṃ \+ yutte, 3\]

Assaraṃ kho byañjanaṃ adhoṭhitaṃ parakkharaṃ naye yutte.

Tatrābhiratim iccheyya (Khu. i, 29).

**Yutte** ti kasmā? Akkocchi maṃ avadhi maṃ, ajini maṃ ahāsi me (Khu. i, 26). Ettha pana yuttaṃ na hoti.

---

When appropriate, one should carry \[the consonant\] to the following \[letter\].

When appropriate, one should carry the consonant, which is without a vowel and which lies below, to the following letter.

Example: “Tatrābhiratim iccheyya”.

Why say “when appropriate” (yutte)? \[To show that in\] “Akkocchi maṃ avadhi maṃ, ajini maṃ ahāsi me”, here, however, it is not appropriate \[to carry the consonant\].[^7]

---

**Iti sandhi-kappe paṭhamo kaṇḍo**

**Thus ends the first section of the chapter on sandhi**

**12\. 13\. Sarā sare lopaṃ (30).** \[Sarā \+ sare \+ lopaṃ, 3\]

Sarā kho sare pare {lopaṃ|elision} papponti.

Yass’ indriyāni samathaṅgatāni (Khu. i, 27, 358), no h’ etaṃ bhante (Vin. iii, 19), samet’ āyasmā saṃghena (Vin. i, 265).

---

When a vowel follows, vowels \[come to\] elision.

When a vowel follows, vowels come to elision.[^8]

Examples: “Yass’ indriyāni samathaṅgatāni”, “no h’ etaṃ bhante”, “samet’ āyasmā saṃghena”.

---

**Formation of words from this and previous suttas.**

**Tatrāyam ādi***.* In order to form this word (actually a combination of two words), first the word must be separated as “tatra ayamādi”. Then by §10 the consonant (here conjunct consonant) ‘tr’ is separated from the vowel ‘a’, and put on the left (tatr a ayamādi); by §12 the ‘a’ is elided because of the following vowel ‘a’ (tatr ayamādi); by §15 the ‘a’ of “ayamādi” is made long (tatr āyamādi); now the consonant ‘tr’ is carried to (or combined with) the following ‘ā’ by §11. Now the word **tatrāyamādi** is finished.

The formation of the word given above is the traditional method of forming words. Students have to follow this procedure when they study Pāḷi grammar and form words. Every example in the book has to be formed formally in this way.

In Oriental scripts, consonants and vowels are written in such a way that they do not appear separately as in Roman script. Therefore in order to do something to the consonant and also the vowel, they have to be separated first and written separately. Only then can one do whatever is appropriate. But in Roman script there is no need to separate the consonant from the vowel since they stand separately and not one on top of the other or the two blended together. The combined words should, however, be separated into two by inserting a space.

**Tatrābhiratim iccheyya** should be treated the same way as “tatrāyamādi”.

Sequence:

1. tatra abhiratimiccheyya  
2. tatr a abhiratimiccheyya (§10)  
3. tatr abhiratimiccheyya (§12)  
4. tatr ābhiratimiccheyya (§15)  
5. tatrābhiratimiccheyya (§11)  
6. tatrābhiratim iccheyya (ME)[^9]

**Yass’ indriyāni***.* In order to form this word, first the word must be written down as “yassa indriyāni”.

Sequence:

1. yassa indriyāni  
2. yass a indriyāni (§10)  
3. yass indriyāni (§12)  
4. yassindriyāni (§11)  
5. yass’ indriyāni (ME)

**No h’ etaṃ***.*

Sequence:

1. no hi etaṃ  
2. no h i etaṃ (§10)  
3. no h etaṃ (§12)  
4. nohetaṃ (§11)  
5. no h’ etaṃ (ME)

**Samet’ āyasmā***.*

Sequence:

1. sametu āyasmā  
2. samet u āyasmā (§10)  
3. samet āyasmā (§12)  
4. sametāyasmā (§11)  
5. samet’ āyasmā (ME)

**13\. 15\. Vā paro asarūpā (31).** \[Vā \+ paro \+ a-sarūpā, 3\]

Saramhā asarūpā paro saro {lopaṃ|elision} pappoti vā.

Cattāro ’me bhikkhave dhammā (A. i, 311), kinnu ’mā ’va samaṇiyo (Vin. iii, 304).

---

Optionally, the following \[vowel is elided\] after a dissimilar \[vowel\].

Optionally, the vowel after a dissimilar vowel comes to elision.[^10]

Examples: “Cattāro ’me bhikkhave dhammā”, “kinnu ’mā ’va samaṇiyo”.

Why say “optionally” (vā)? \[To prevent elision of the following vowel, as in the following examples\] “Pañc’ indriyāni”; “tay’ assu dhammā jahitā bhavanti” \[although they fall in the scope of this sutta\].

**18\. 20\. Vam od-udantānaṃ (44).** \[Vaṃ \+ o-u-dantānaṃ, 2\]

Other examples beginning with these should be formed.

**30\. 58\. Aṃ byañjane niggahitaṃ (153).** \[Aṃ \+ byañjane \+ niggahitaṃ, 3\]

Niggahitaṃ kho byañjane pare aṃ iti hoti.

Evaṃ vutte (Vin. i, 7), taṃ sādhū ti paṭissuṇitvā (DhA. i, 30).

---

When a consonant follows, the niggahita ‘ṃ’.

When a consonant follows, the niggahita becomes ‘ṃ’.[^22]

Examples: “Evaṃ vutte”, “taṃ sādhū ti paṭissuṇitvā”.

---

Examples \[with formation sequence\]:

**Evaṃ vutte**: evaṃ vutte (§30)

**Taṃ sādhū ti**: taṃ sādhū ti (§30)

**31\. 49\. Vaggantaṃ vā vagge (138-9).** \[Vagga-antaṃ \+ vā \+ vagge, 3\]

Vaggabhūte byañjane pare {niggahitaṃ|niggahita (nasalization)} kho vaggantaṃ vā pappoti.

Tan niccutaṃ, dhammañ care sucaritaṃ (Khu. i, 39), cirappavāsiṃ purisaṃ (Khu. i, 45).  
 Santan tassa manaṃ hoti (Khu. i, 27), taṅ kāruṇikaṃ, evaṅ kho bhikkhave sikkhitabbaṃ.

Vāggahaṇena niggahitaṃ kho lakārādeso hoti. Puggalaṃ.[^23]

**Vā** ti kasmā? Na taṃ kammaṃ kataṃ sādhu (Khu. i, 23).

---

Optionally, when a grouped \[consonant\] follows, the end of the group.

When a grouped consonant follows, the niggahita optionally becomes the last letter of the corresponding group.

Examples: “Tan niccutaṃ”, “dhammañ care sucaritaṃ”, “cirappavāsiṃ purisaṃ”, “santan tassa manaṃ hoti”, “taṅ kāruṇikaṃ”, “evaṅ kho bhikkhave sikkhitabbaṃ”.

---

Examples \[with formation sequence\]:

**Tan niccutaṃ**: taṃ niccutaṃ; tan niccutaṃ (§31); tanniccutaṃ (§11); tan niccutaṃ (ME).

**Dhammañ care**: dhammaṃ care; dhammañ care (§31); dhammañcare (§11); dhammañ care (ME).

**Cirappavāsiṃ**: ciraṃ pavāsiṃ; ciram pavāsiṃ (§31); cirap pavāsiṃ (by “ca” in §35); cirappavāsiṃ (§11).

**Santan tassa**: santaṃ tassa; santan tassa (§31); santantassa (§11); santan tassa (ME).

**Taṅ kāruṇikaṃ**: taṃ kāruṇikaṃ; taṅ kāruṇikaṃ (§31); taṅkāruṇikaṃ (§11); taṅ kāruṇikaṃ (ME).

**Evaṅ kho: evaṃ kho**; evaṅ kho (§31); evaṅkho (§11); evaṅ kho (ME).

---

**Extension by “vā” (optional):** By taking “optionally”, there is also substitution of the niggahita by ‘l’.

**Puggalaṃ**: puggaṃ aṃ; puggal aṃ (by “vā” in §31); puggalaṃ (§11).[^24]

Why say “optionally”? \[To prevent the operation of this rule in the following example\] “Na taṃ kammaṃ kataṃ sādhu”.

**32\. 50\. E-he ñaṃ (140).** \[E-he \+ ñaṃ, 2\]

**E**kāra-**ha**kāre pare {niggahitaṃ|niggahita (nasalization)} kho **ña**kāraṃ pappoti vā.

Paccattañ ñeva parinibbāyissāmi (M. i, 318), tañ ñev' ettha paṭipucchissāmi (D. i, 57), evañ hi vo bhikkhave sikkhitabbaṃ (M. i, 171). Tañ hi tassa musā hoti (Khu. i, 396).

**Vā** ti kasmā? Evam etaṃ abhiññāya (Khu. i, 447), evaṃ hoti subhāsitaṃ.

---

When ‘e’ and ‘h’, ‘ñ’.

When the letters ‘e’ and ‘h’ follow, the niggahita optionally becomes ‘ñ’.[^25]

Examples: “Paccattañ ñeva parinibbāyissāmi”, “tañ ñev' ettha paṭipucchissāmi”, “evañ hi vo bhikkhave sikkhitabbaṃ”, “tañ hi tassa musā hoti”.

---

Examples \[with formation sequence\]:

**Paccattañ ñeva**: paccattaṃ eva; paccattañ eva (§32); paccattaññ eva (§28); paccattaññeva (§11); paccattañ ñeva (ME).

**Tañ ñev’ ettha**: taṃ eva ettha; tañ ev’ ettha (§32); taññ ev’ ettha (§28); taññevettha (§11); tañ ñev’ ettha (ME).

**Evañ hi**: evaṃ hi; evañ hi (§32); evañhi (§11); evañ hi (ME).

**Tañ hi*:*** taṃ hi; tañ hi (§32); tañhi (§11); tañ hi (ME).

---

Why say “optionally”? \[To prevent the operation of this rule in the following examples\] “Evam etaṃ abhiññāya”, “evaṃ hoti subhāsitaṃ”.

Counter-examples:

**Evam etaṃ***.* Here the ‘ṃ’ of “evaṃ” is not followed by “eva”.

**Evaṃ hoti***.* Here ‘ṃ’ is not followed by “hi”.

**33\. 51\. Sa ye ca (141).** \[Sa \+ ye \+ ca, 3\]

Niggahitaṃ kho **ya**kāre pare saha **ya**kārena **ña**kāraṃ pappoti vā. 

Saññogo, saññuttaṃ. 

**Vā** ti kasmā? Saṃyogo, saṃyuttaṃ.

---

And when ‘y’, together with \[ṃ\].

When the letter ‘y’ follows, the niggahita together with the letter ‘y’ optionally becomes ‘ñ’.

Examples: “Saññogo”, “saññuttaṃ”.

---

Examples \[with formation sequence\]:

**Saññogo**: saṃ yogo; sañ ñogo (§33); saññogo (§11) (ME).

**Saññuttaṃ**: saṃ yuttaṃ; sañ ñuttaṃ (§33); saññuttaṃ (§11) (ME).

---

Why say “optionally”? \[To prevent the operation of this rule in the following examples\] “Saṃyogo”, “saṃyuttaṃ”.

---

Counter-examples:

**Saṃyogo***.* Here there are two choices, “saññogo” and “saṃyogo”; but here the speaker chooses to say “saṃyogo”. Therefore ‘ṃ’ together with ‘y’ is not changed to ‘ñ’.

**Saṃyuttaṃ**. To be understood similarly as “saṃyogo”.

Why say “optionally”? \[To prevent the operation of this rule in the following examples\] “Akkocchi maṃ avadhi maṃ”, “ajini maṃ ahāsi me”.

Counter-examples:

**Maṃ ahāsi***.* Here the niggahita is not changed to ‘m’.

**35\. 34\. Ya-va-ma-da-na-ta-ra-lā c’ āgamā (56).** \[Ya-va-ma-da-na-ta-ra-lā \+ ca \+ āgamā, 3\]

Sare pare **ya**kāro **va**kāro **ma**kāro **da**kāro **na**kāro **ta**kāro **ra**kāro **la**kāro ime {āgamā|inserted letters} honti vā.

Asso bhadro kasā-m-iva (Khu. i, 34), samma-d-aññā vimuttānaṃ (Khu. i, 21). Manasā-d-aññā vimuttānaṃ, atta-d-attham abhiññāya (Khu. i, 38). Ciraṃ-n-āyati, ito-n-āyati. Yasmā-t-iha bhikkhave, tasmā-t-iha bhikkhave (S. i, 267), ajja-t-agge (D. i, 80) pāṇupetaṃ. Sabbhi-r-eva samāsetha (S. i, 16), āragge-r-iva sāsapo (Khu. i, 71),[^26] sāsapo-r-iva āraggā (Khu. i, 71). Cha-ḷ-abhiññā (Khu. iv, 322), sa-ḷ-āyatanaṃ (Vin. iii, 1).

Vā ti kasmā? Evaṃ mahiddhiyā esā, akkocchi maṃ avadhi maṃ (Khu. i, 13), ajini maṃ ahāsi me (Khu. i, 13), ajeyyo anugāmiko (Khu. i, 10).

**Ca**ggahaṇena idh’ eva **ma**kārassa **pa**kāro hoti. Cirappavāsiṃ purisaṃ (Khu. i, 45).

**Ka**kārassa ca **da**kāro hoti. Sadatthapasuto siyā (Khu. i, 38).

**Da**kārassa ca **ta**kāro hoti. Sugato (Vin. i, 1).

And the insertions ‘y’, ‘v’, ‘m’, ‘d’, ‘n’, ‘t’, ‘r’, and ‘l’.

---

**Yatha-y-idaṃ**: yathā idaṃ; yathā y idaṃ (§35); yatha y idaṃ (§26); yathayidaṃ (§11); yatha-y-idaṃ (ME).[^27]

**Bhantā-v-udikkhati**, **lahu-m-essati**, **garu-m-essati**, **kasā-m-iva**: the formation is similar to the previous ones.

**41\. 56\. Byañjano ca visaññogo (135-6, 149).** \[Byañjano \+ ca \+ vi-saññogo, 3\]

Niggahitamhā parasmiṃ sare lutte yadi byañjano sasaññogo visaññogo hoti.

Evaṃ ’sa te āsavā (M. i, 13), pupphaṃ ’sā uppajji (Vin. i, 21).

Lutte ti kasmā? Evam assa (Vin. i, 265), vidūn' aggam iti.

---

And the consonant, single.

When the vowel that follows the niggahita is elided, if the syllable has a conjunct consonant, it becomes a single consonant.

Examples: “Evaṃ ’sa te āsavā”, “pupphaṃ ’sā uppajji”.

---

Examples \[with formation sequence\]:

**Evaṃ ’sa**: evaṃ assa; evaṃ ssa (§40); evaṃ sa (§41); evaṃsa (§11); evaṃ ’sa (ME).

**Pupphaṃ ’sā**: pupphaṃ assā; pupphaṃ ssā (§40); pupphaṃ sā (§41); pupphaṃsā (§11); pupphaṃ ’sā (ME).

---

Why say “when \[the vowel\] has been elided” (*lutte*)? \[To prevent the operation of this rule in the following examples\] “Evam assa”, “vidūn’ aggam iti”.

---

Counter-examples:

**Evam assa**. Here and in the following example there is no elision of the vowel that follows the niggahita. Since there is no elision of the ‘a’s of “assa” and “aggaṃ”, the conjunct consonants ‘ss’ and ‘gg’ do not become single consonants, ‘s’ and ‘g’.

**Vidūn’ aggam***.*

---

**Extension by “ca” (accumulating):** By taking “ca”, when there are three consonants, there is also elision of (the beginning consonant of) those consonants that are similar.

**Agyāgāraṃ**: aggi āgāraṃ; aggy āgāraṃ (§21); agy āgāraṃ (“ca” of §41); agyāgāraṃ (§11).

**Paṭisanthāravuty assa**: paṭisanthāravutti assa; paṭisanthāravutty assa (§21); paṭisanthāravuty assa (“ca” of §41); paṭisanthāravutyassa (§11); paṭisanthāravuty assa (ME).
